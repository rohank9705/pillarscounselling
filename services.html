Types of Therapy

Choosing therapy can feel overwhelming, especially if you are unsure which approach may be right for you. Different therapeutic approaches offer different ways of understanding experiences, creating change, and supporting personal growth.

At Pillars Counselling Services, my main therapeutic approach is Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT). However, I recognise that therapy is personal, and the most suitable approach will depend on your individual needs, preferences, and goals. Below is an overview of some common therapeutic approaches to help you explore the different options available.

Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT)

CBT is commonly used to support difficulties such as anxiety, depression, stress, phobias, obsessive thoughts, and managing the impact of long-term health conditions.

Person-Centred Therapy

Through a genuine, accepting, and supportive therapeutic relationship, this approach provides a space where you can explore your potential, build self-awareness, and work towards becoming a more fulfilled version of yourself.

Psychodynamic Therapy

Psychodynamic Therapy explores how past experiences, relationships, and deeper emotional patterns may influence the way we experience life in the present.

Other Therapeutic Approaches

Pluralistic Counselling

Pluralistic Counselling recognises that there is no single approach to therapy that works for everyone. It focuses on working collaboratively with you to understand your individual needs, preferences, and goals.

The therapist may draw from different therapeutic approaches and adapt the support provided depending on what feels most helpful for you.

Humanistic Therapy

Humanistic therapy focuses on the individual as a whole and emphasises personal growth, self-development, and reaching your potential.

This approach supports individuals in developing self-awareness, recognising their own strengths, and taking responsibility for their choices and personal journey.

Eye Movement Desensitisation and Reprocessing (EMDR)

EMDR is an approach often used to support individuals who have experienced distressing or traumatic events, particularly those experiencing symptoms associated with post-traumatic stress.

EMDR aims to help the brain process difficult memories so that they become less overwhelming and easier to manage.

Existential Therapy

Existential Therapy explores some of the deeper questions and challenges of life, such as identity, meaning, choice, responsibility, and how we find purpose.

This approach can support individuals in exploring their values, making meaningful decisions, and developing a greater understanding of their own life journey.
